# Pilot Metro Research — Directory22 Region #1 (Oct 2026)

Goal: pick the first metro for a rank-and-rent home-services directory
(HVAC/plumbing focus). Must be winnable on SEO within months, big enough to
monetize, and practical for signing the first local pros.

## 1. Selection criteria

From rank-and-rent practitioner sources:

- **Real local search volume for the niche.** Population alone doesn't prove
  it — check "[service] + [city]" volume and CPC. Practitioner rule of thumb:
  city pop 10k–400k, CPC under $5, moderate competition acceptable, 3–6 months
  to rank. (https://docs.google.com/document/d/1NQD5WOpo2_QV4jqQA9adeCS4VFRY72gc77KgALaS2JI/mobilebasic)
- **Few existing rank-and-rent sites already ranking.** Audit the SERP: generic
  lead-capture sites with no physical presence = someone already claimed it;
  established local businesses = still room (or harder — dig further).
  (https://medium.com/@maximus.ranson/the-best-cities-for-rank-and-rent-lists-are-making-you-less-money-07231176b549)
- **Enough business density to sell to.** Too small = you rank but have nobody
  to rent the lead flow to.
  (https://medium.com/@maximus.ranson/the-best-cities-for-rank-and-rent-lists-are-making-you-less-money-07231176b549)
- **Phone-driven niche, short sales cycle, ~$1K average ticket.** HVAC/plumbing
  qualify. (https://docs.google.com/document/d/1NQD5WOpo2_QV4jqQA9adeCS4VFRY72gc77KgALaS2JI/mobilebasic)
- **Older housing stock = more service calls.** Furnaces, water heaters, and
  pipes fail on age. New-construction-heavy metros generate fewer repair calls
  per household.
- **High homeownership.** Owners call plumbers; renters call landlords.
- **Growth + income.** Growing population = new customers; solid household
  income = pros can afford to pay for leads and homeowners can afford the work.

### Read on each

- **Minneapolis–St. Paul:** biggest prize, oldest homes (49–56 yrs = maximum
  repair demand), 75% owner-occupancy. But it's the hardest SEO fight in the
  set and the slowest pilot. Save it for region #4–5, when the domain has
  authority and the playbook is proven.
- **Des Moines:** the balanced pick. 759k people = real search volume;
  fastest-growing major Midwest metro; A+ affordability with $86.7k median
  household income (Realtor.com 2026 metro report cards); SERPs are
  beatable — Thumbtack/Porch listicles plus local companies, no dominant
  directory. Building boom means newer suburbs, but the urban core stock is
  older. (https://www.realtor.com/news/trends/homebuilding-affordability-des-moines-iowa-2026-report-card/)
- **Rochester, MN:** smallest, softest competition, affluent Mayo-anchored
  economy, 74% regional homeownership. Weaknesses: only ~229k metro (thin
  volume for a full directory) and young housing stock (~36 yrs). Best as a
  fast second region or a home-field play (see §3).
- **Boise:** growth story is real, but the youngest housing stock in America
  (~24 yrs median) structurally suppresses per-household repair demand. Wrong
  shape for a repair-led directory pilot.
- **Colorado Springs:** strong runner-up. Aging stock (1986 median build),
  growth, $87.8k incomes, hot market. The military transient population means
  churn — good for property-manager angles, slightly weaker for
  owner-occupied repair demand. More SEO attention than Midwest markets.
- **Sioux Falls:** the "easy mode" pick — 67% homeownership, 2%/yr growth,
  soft competition. But ~308k metro caps the prize; better as region #2/#3
  than the pilot that proves the model.

## 3. Home-field advantage

**For launching where the founder lives or has connections:**

- **Local content is a ranking factor, not a nice-to-have.** The replication
  playbook requires 60%+ unique local content per page with first-party proof
  (local stats, real photos, landmarks, named businesses) — Google's 2025
  SpamBrain upgrade crushed cookie-cutter city pages. A founder who knows the
  metro can produce genuine local proof 10x faster than one researching it
  remotely.
- **Face-to-face pro sign-ups.** The pilot needs 1–3 pros on pay-per-lead or
  flat monthly. Walking into shops with a live demo beats cold outreach, and
  local reputation compounds.
- **Against:** pure data-optimal markets can outperform if the founder commits
  to remote content production (hire a local writer/photographer, interview
  pros on Zoom). The content bar is clear and outsourceable — it's just more
  work.

**Net:** home-field is worth roughly one tier of market-size tradeoff. If the
founder has real Minnesota ties, Rochester becomes a defensible pilot (fast
win, local proof easy, then clone the playbook to Des Moines). Without
home-field, pick the data winner.

## 4. Recommendation

**Archetype: mid-size (500k–1M), growing, affordable Midwest metro with
mixed-age housing stock.** Big enough to monetize, small enough to rank in
months, overlooked enough that national SEO competition is thin.

**Start HERE: Des Moines–West Des Moines, IA.**
- 758,539 people and the fastest-growing major metro in the Midwest —
  volume plus momentum.
- A+ affordability, $86.7k median household income — pros can pay, homeowners
  can buy.
- Beatable SERPs: Thumbtack/Porch listicles and local companies, no
  entrenched directory, no rank-and-rent incumbent visible.
- Midwest market = less national SEO attention than Sun Belt boomtowns.
- Older urban-core housing + new suburbs = repair demand today, growth
  demand tomorrow.

**Runner-up: Colorado Springs, CO** (aging stock + growth + income; pick it
if the founder prefers the Mountain West or wants the property-manager
angle via the military population).

**Save for later:** Minneapolis–St. Paul (biggest prize, hardest fight —
region #4–5 once the domain has authority); Rochester/Sioux Falls (fast,
small wins — regions #2–3, or region #1 only with genuine home-field
advantage). **Skip for pilot:** Boise (youngest housing stock in the US =
structurally low repair demand per household).

**Gates before region #2** (from the launch plan): indexed pages + one paying
pro in Des Moines. Then clone the playbook.
